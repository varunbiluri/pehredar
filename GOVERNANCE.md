# Governance

Pehredar uses a lightweight maintainer-led governance model suitable for its
current size, with a documented path toward shared ownership.

## Roles

- **Contributors** participate through issues, discussions, reviews, code,
  documentation, testing, translation, or design.
- **Reviewers** are trusted contributors who regularly review a defined area.
- **Maintainers** merge changes, manage releases and security reports, enforce
  community standards, and make project-wide decisions.

The current lead maintainer is `@varunbiluri`.

## Decision making

Routine decisions happen through pull-request review. Significant changes—new
permissions, network access, data collection, architecture changes, supported
platforms, license changes, or security-policy changes—require a public design
issue and explicit maintainer approval. Privacy and user safety outweigh feature
velocity. When consensus is not possible, maintainers document the decision and
rationale publicly, except for embargoed security matters.

## Becoming a reviewer or maintainer

Sustained, constructive contributions and sound judgment are the basis for
additional responsibility. A maintainer may nominate a contributor after
several meaningful contributions. The nomination, scope, and decision are
recorded publicly. Access may be removed for inactivity, security reasons, or
code-of-conduct violations after notice when practical.

## Conflicts of interest

Decision makers disclose relevant financial, employment, or personal conflicts
and recuse when appropriate. No contributor may approve their own high-risk
change without independent review once multiple maintainers exist.

## Project continuity

If the lead maintainer becomes unavailable for 90 days, active reviewers may
coordinate publicly to nominate a successor while preserving the license,
history, privacy principles, and contributor attribution.
